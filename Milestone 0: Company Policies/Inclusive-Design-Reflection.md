# Reflection

## How can you adjust your communication style to be more inclusive of neurodivergent users and teammates?
I can try to communicate clearly and directly instead of being vague or giving too much information at once. I can also be patient if someone needs more time to respond or prefers communicating in a different way, such as through written messages instead of calls.

## What are some common UX or communication pitfalls that might make Focus Bear less accessible or supportive?
Having too much information on the screen, unclear instructions, too many notifications or complicated navigation could make the app overwhelming. Additionally, communication that sounds judgemental or makes users feel guilty for not completing tasks could also be discouraging for people struggling with executive functioning.

## What is one practical change you can make in your work to better support vulnerable populations?
One practical change I can make is to keep interfaces and instructions simple and clear, especially when working on features that require users to make decisions or complete several steps.

# Task

## Read a first-person account from someone with ADHD or Autism about their experiences with productivity tools. (Hint: Look for blog posts, videos, or community discussions.)
I read a discussion from someone with ADHD who described becoming overwhelmed by productivity apps with too many features and information. They found that large tasks could cause task paralysis and preferred tools that broke tasks into smaller steps and reduced unnecessary distractions. As someone who is also auDHD, I agree that having minimal features and optimising the accessibility of core features of the app can help with task paralysis and create higher usage of the the platform.

## Identify one design or communication improvement that could make Focus Bear more accessible.
Focus Bear could make tasks easier to start by allowing larger tasks to be broken down into small and manageable steps, while keeping the interface simple and avoiding unnecessary information or distractions. 

## Practice writing a clear, patient, and supportive response to a hypothetical user struggling with executive functioning.
If a user was struggling to start a task, I could respond with:
"It might help to make the first step as small and clear as possible. Instead of trying to complete the whole task at once, you could start with one small action and work from there. You can also use Focus Bear to break your work into shorter focus sessions and take breaks when needed."
