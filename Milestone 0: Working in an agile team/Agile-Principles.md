# Reflection

## What do you think are the biggest benefits and challenges of Agile?
I think one of the biggest benefits of Agile is flexibility. Software requirements are rarely completely fixed, so being able to change direction based on feedback or new information reduces the risk of spending a long time developing something that is no longer useful. Agile is also used to encourages communication between developers and other members of the team, which can help clarify requirements and prevent misunderstandings.

One challenge of the Agile structure is that it relies heavily on good communication and self-management. If tasks are unclear or team members do not communicate blockers, work can become disorganised. 

## How can Agile principles help improve your role and responsibilities?
As a developer, Agile principles can help me focus on completing smaller, useful pieces of work rather than trying to solve everything at once. For example, when I am assigned a github issue, I can concentrate on understanding and completing that task, testing my changes, and getting feedback through code review before moving on to another issue.


# Task

## Summarize the main differences between Scrum and Kanban in your own words.
The biggest difference is that Scrum organises work around time, while Kanban organises work around flow. Scrum asks the team to select a group of tasks and work on them during a fixed sprint. Kanban allows tasks to move continuously through the workflow, with developers taking on new work as capacity becomes available. Scrum also has more formally defined roles and ceremonies, while Kanban is generally less prescriptive. 


## Identify one Agile principle you think will be most useful in your work.
The Agile principle I think will be most useful in my work is:
“At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behaviour accordingly.”

I think this is particularly useful as an intern because I will inevitably make mistakes or find better ways of approaching tasks as I gain experience. Instead of expecting myself to know the best approach immediately, I can use feedback from stand-ups and completed tasks to improve how I work.
